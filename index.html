Hill cipher, published by Lester S. Hill in 1929, encrypts blocks of n letters at once by treating them as a vector and multiplying by an n×n key matrix mod 26. It's the first practical polygraphic substitution cipher — and, like all classical ciphers, offers no security against modern cryptanalysis. Educational use only.
